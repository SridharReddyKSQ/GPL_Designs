2  Purpose, audience and method
2.1  Purpose
This Business Requirements Document defines what the Unified Digital Platform must do for Guyana Power & Light in Phase 1. It translates the contracted Phase 1 scope into numbered, testable business requirements, elaborates each one so that it can be designed and tested without further interpretation, records the assumptions made where information was not available, and lists the decisions GPL must take before build can proceed.
2.2  Audience
GPL Programme Sponsor, Programme Manager and business subject matter experts, who approve the requirements; EK Digital delivery, architecture and quality assurance, who build and test against them; and the programme management office, which manages scope and change control against the fixed-price engagement.
2.3  Method
Requirements were derived from the contracted Phase 1 scope and validated with GPL through the discovery workshops held in August 2026. The contracted scope governs: where a business need was raised that has no Phase 1 line item, it is recorded as out of scope in section 3.3 rather than as a requirement. Where a Phase 1 capability was not covered in the workshops, an assumption is recorded and the requirement is marked accordingly. Operating facts established in the workshops are recorded unchanged in section 5.2 and are the basis of the elaboration attached to each requirement.
2.4  How to read a requirement
Each requirement carries an identifier, the requirement statement, an elaboration and an acceptance criterion, together with a MoSCoW priority and a status.
Requirement — the statement that is approved. Statements carried forward from version 1.0 are unchanged.
Detail — how the requirement behaves in practice: the business rules, the fields, the current-state position it replaces, and the boundaries.
Acceptance — how it will be demonstrated as delivered. This is the basis of the test coverage in section 14.
Priority — Must: required for Phase 1 go-live. Should: required unless it threatens the go-live date. Could: desirable, delivered if capacity allows.
Status — Confirmed: evidenced and not in dispute. Assumed: specified on an assumption recorded in section 16. Open: dependent on a decision or an input recorded in section 17.

5.2  Operating facts recorded in discovery
These are the facts established with GPL during discovery, recorded unchanged. They are the basis of the elaboration attached to each requirement and of the assumptions in section 16.
[TABLE]
| Area | Fact as established
| Intake channels | Customers report by telephone to the call centre, by WhatsApp message, through Live Chat on the website, or by walking into a commercial office. Email is not accepted — the call centre does not receive complaints by email. There is no customer portal and no mobile application.
| Unlogged channels | Around fifteen or more outage reports arrive every day through social media channels that Customer Service does not monitor. None of them are logged, counted, dispatched or reported.
| Emergency database | Incidents are captured in a standalone emergency database, separate from CIS. Nothing entered there reaches the customer record automatically. There are no dashboards; supervisors log in and look.
| Reference numbering | On save, a reference number is generated and read back to the customer. The series is sequential and is currently in the 3000 range.
| Two identifiers | The call centre works with reference numbers in the emergency database while the rest of GPL works with CIS service orders. Nothing joins the two.
| Customer identification | Customers are identified by the address given on the call. Two different customers can share a customer number, a telephone number and an address.
| Intake data set | The operator captures customer name, contact number, street and area, and the nature of the fault. No issue type, severity or asset reference is captured.
| Issue types | GPL has supplied a routing matrix of 92 incident and issue types, each with a priority from P1 to P5, an impact scope, and an indicative routing across logging, validation, first team, follow-on team and back-office closure.
| Duplicate handling | Only one reference is kept per incident. Duplicate spotting depends on the operator recognising the street from an earlier call in the same shift.
| Dispatch | Jobs are dispatched by telephone call plus a post in a WhatsApp group. The crew acknowledges in the group. There is no record of when the crew was assigned, no acknowledgement timestamp, and no view of which crews are free.
| Field completion | Crews report completion by telephone, by WhatsApp, or through an ODK form. Contractors always use ODK; GPL crews increasingly do. Connectivity is unreliable and location is not always captured.
| Re-keying | Completion detail — arrival time, duration on the job, what the fault was, and comments — is typed into the emergency database by a customer service representative, hours after the event.
| Monitoring | Monitoring is manual. Supervisors log into the call centre database to see jobs coming in and follow up by telephone, roughly every four hours during a shift.
| Escalation | If a crew has not attended within about two hours, the call centre contacts the crew, then the Superintendent on shift, then the senior assistant personnel; work not completed within twelve hours escalates to the manager for that workforce. Nothing escalates above manager level. The chain is described as personal practice, not a documented standard.
| Shift pattern and handover | Field work runs three shifts — 07:00 to 15:00, 15:00 to 23:00 and 23:00 to 07:00. At the end of each shift the outgoing team leader hands a paper document listing outstanding jobs to the incoming leader.
| Volumes | Zone 1 alone receives about 1,200 emergency jobs per month. Company-wide volume has not been provided.
| Service requests | Non-outage requests are raised as service orders in CIS. Everything moves via a service order, routing depends on personal knowledge of the rules, and the order is closed with comments. There is no SLA, no resolution code and no status the customer can see.
| Control room visibility | System Control sees 13.8 kV and above only. Faults below the substation are invisible to the control room, which does not know about them unless the call centre telephones.
| Control room recording | A feeder trip is recorded automatically in SCADA, written by hand into a paper logbook, entered again into an ODK form at the substation, and then transcribed the next morning into an Excel file that performs analysis and is emailed to the distribution list once a day.
| SCADA estate | GPL runs two SCADA systems in parallel — a legacy system whose control-centre historian is down and cannot be restored, and a newer system that is recording. The newer system carries a reporting application through which dashboards can be published to named users.
| SCADA interface constraint | GPL has confirmed there is no two-way communication with SCADA. Information is pushed out only and none is accepted in, for cyber-security reasons. Mixing other communications onto the SCADA network was refused. A periodic file transmitted to a central location was accepted as a possibility.
| Field devices | Reclosers, sectionalisers and fault current indicators are installed and are capable of communicating, but the infrastructure to report back to the control system has not been built out. Substation devices communicate over fibre using the IEC 104 protocol.
| Feeder and area data | Customer Service and the call centre use a manual document, the area guide, which spells out which feeder supplies which area. It becomes out of date whenever a feeder is switched. Feeder-level customer counts do not exist digitally and are reconstructed by hand from CIS when required.
| Address quality | Addresses do not state which side of the street a premise is on, and two feeders can run along the same street, so a customer cannot reliably be resolved to a feeder from the address alone.
| GIS position | There is no corporate GIS. Switching-device coordinates have been captured on foot in one zone and are being plotted in a consumer mapping tool; an older mapping of 13.8 kV routes exists for part of the network and is out of date; a GIS unit within IT reportedly holds customer coordinates, feeder layouts, smart device locations, transformers and network defects.
| Reporting | Operational reporting is compiled by hand each month from the call centre database and from spreadsheets. There is no MTTR, no SLA attainment and no ageing measure, and no baseline exists.
| Restoration confirmation | Restoration is confirmed verbally, by radio or in a WhatsApp group. The restore time is written in the control room logbook and later transcribed into the daily spreadsheet.
| Estimated restoration time | There is no estimated restoration time field anywhere. When the call centre needs one it telephones the control room and is given a verbal answer.
[/TABLE]

6  Future state overview
The future state is documented in the two process flows issued with this pack. GPL (Phase 1) · Incident and Outage Management covers the whole customer-and-operations journey as one flow: intake on any channel, classification, deduplication, the outage lifecycle through to restoration and closure, dispatch and field execution, the non-outage service request workflow, automated customer notifications, portal and mobile self-service, and the network event feed. GPL (Phase 1) · Fuel Consumption Management covers the fuel consumption entry screen, which shares no personas, integrations or triggers with the rest of Phase 1.
Six changes carry most of the Phase 1 benefit:
One case, created once. Every channel — portal, application, telephone, WhatsApp, email and walk-in — produces a single case in one system, replacing the emergency database reference and the separate control-room record.
Identification before description. The representative asks for the account number and CIS returns the customer, so the agent verifies rather than re-types and the case is tied to the right account from the first second.
Work orders instead of messages. Validation creates the service order, work order and service appointment automatically, and dispatch happens on a console showing crew availability rather than through a telephone call and a group chat.
Capture at source. The crew records en route, on site, actions, materials, photographs and closure on the mobile application, so timestamps are real and nothing is re-typed.
The clock, not the complaint. Service levels and escalation run on elapsed time from logging, visible on the console and the dashboards, with a documented escalation matrix behind them.
The customer is told. Notifications fire from the case on every material status change, and the portal and application show status and estimated restoration without an agent.

7.1  Incident and outage intake (omni-channel)
Standardised intake for outages and incidents from contact centre, walk-in, portal and email as applicable; case classification by type, severity, feeder or area and asset; validations and deduplication rules; linking incidents to customer, location and asset or GIS references where available. Deliverables: configured outage and incident case model and forms; classification and validation rules; intake flows and case creation channels.
[TABLE]
| ID | Requirement, elaboration and acceptance | Priority | Status
| BR-INT-001 | The platform shall provide a single intake process that produces one case irrespective of the channel through which the report arrives. Detail  One case object with an incident record type serves every channel. The record layout presented to internal users is identical whether the case was created by an agent, by the portal, by the mobile application, by email-to-case or from a network event, so that no team works from a channel-specific view. This replaces the present position in which the call centre works in a standalone emergency database while the rest of GPL works on CIS service orders. Acceptance  A case created on each of the six channels is opened in the console and shows the same layout, the same mandatory fields and the same status set. | Must | Confirmed
| BR-INT-002 | Intake shall be supported from inbound telephone call, walk-in, customer portal, mobile application, email-to-case and WhatsApp. Case creation from telephone is subject to CTI availability. Detail  Telephone and walk-in are agent-created. Portal and mobile application are customer-created. Email-to-case requires a monitored mailbox nominated by GPL. WhatsApp is bidirectional — inbound messages create or update a case and outbound notifications use the same channel. Where CTI is available, an inbound call presents the matched customer to the agent; where it is not, the agent searches manually and telephone intake is otherwise unchanged. Acceptance  Each channel is demonstrated end to end in user acceptance testing, and the channel of origin is recorded on every case. | Must | Open — Q-09
| BR-INT-003 | A report submitted through the portal or the mobile application by a signed-in customer shall create the case automatically, already linked to that customer's account. Detail  No agent intervention is required. The account, contact, service address and contact details are taken from the authenticated session, and the nature of the incident, the issue type and any attachment are taken from the guided form. The case enters the same queue as an agent-created case and is subject to the same classification, deduplication and validation rules. Acceptance  A signed-in customer submits a report and the resulting case shows the correct account, premise and contact without an agent touching it. | Must | Confirmed
| BR-INT-004 | On every other channel the case shall be created by the CSR or TSO taking the report. The portal and mobile application are customer channels and are not used by GPL staff to create cases. Detail  This makes the ownership rule explicit: self-service is the customer's channel; every assisted channel produces an agent-owned case. The creating agent becomes the case owner until the case is routed to a queue. Acceptance  Cases created on assisted channels show the creating agent as owner; no internal user holds an authenticated customer portal account. | Must | Confirmed
| BR-INT-005 | When creating a case the CSR or TSO shall request the customer account number and retrieve the account from Infinity CIS, so that customer name, contact number and service address are populated for verification rather than re-typed. Detail  The agent asks for the account number, the platform calls CIS, and the returned name, contact number, service address and premise details are displayed for the agent to confirm with the caller. Where the caller does not know the account number, search by telephone number, by name and by address is available as a fallback. This replaces identification by address alone, which today cannot be relied upon because address, telephone number and customer number can all be shared between customers. Acceptance  Given a valid account number, the customer record is retrieved and populated on the case within the agreed response time, and the agent records that identity was verified. | Must | Confirmed
| BR-INT-006 | Where the reporting party cannot be matched to a CIS account the case shall still be created and progressed as an unverified report. Detail  Hazard reports, third-party callers and passers-by have no account. The case is flagged as unverified, carries the reported location and the caller's contact details, and is fully dispatchable. Unverified cases are reportable as a measure of both hazard reporting and customer data quality. Acceptance  A case can be created, classified, dispatched, resolved and closed without a CIS account, and appears in the unverified report count. | Must | Assumed — A-03
| BR-INT-007 | The CSR or TSO shall capture the nature of the incident, the issue type and the severity. All other intake data shall be derived from the account or from the channel. Detail  The agent captures only what the customer alone can supply: what is happening, when it started if known, and any hazard present. Issue type is selected from the catalogue and drives the default severity or priority; the agent may override the default with a reason. Name, contact number, address, premise and account are inherited from the verified account, so the current practice of typing name, contact number, street and area on every call is removed. 9  Service levels, escalation and cycle time
Service levels and escalation are a contracted Phase 1 capability: SLA and OLA tracking with audit history, escalation rules and exception handling, and SLA attainment dashboards. No service level target exists anywhere in GPL today. The only intervals evidenced in discovery are an expectation that a crew attends within about two hours, and an escalation to the responsible manager where work is not complete within twelve hours; both are described as practice rather than as an approved standard, and both are triggered in practice by a customer calling back rather than by a clock. The targets proposed in 9.2 and 9.3 are therefore a starting position for GPL to approve or amend, and are recorded as an assumption.
